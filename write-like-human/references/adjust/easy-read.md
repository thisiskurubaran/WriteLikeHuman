# Easy Read

Turn information into Easy Read: a specific accessible format for people with learning disabilities, and useful for anyone who finds reading hard. It is stricter than simply making text simpler.

This is different from `adjust/reading-level.md`. Reading level makes text easier for a particular reader. Easy Read follows a set format: one idea per sentence, short lines, common words, and a picture next to each point.

## When this applies

- "Make this Easy Read", "Easy Read version", "for someone with a learning disability".
- Notices, letters, consent forms, appointment information, rules, or instructions from schools, clinics, NGOs, care homes, and councils.
- The user mentions a reader with a learning disability, autism, or brain injury who finds standard text hard.

## What stays the same

- Every fact: dates, times, places, names, phone numbers, amounts, and what the reader must do. Easy Read leaves out extra words, not information the reader needs.
- The meaning and any rights or choices the reader has ("you can say no").
- The language. Easy Read in Hindi or Tamil follows the same rules in that language.

## The format

- One idea per sentence. One sentence per line.
- Short sentences, ideally under 15 words.
- Common, everyday words. Explain any hard word the first time, in brackets or the next line.
- No idioms, metaphors, or jokes ("touch base", "on the same page").
- Numbers as digits: "3 days", "10 am", not "three days".
- Active voice and "you": "You need to bring your card."
- Bold the most important words sparingly, not whole sentences.
- Left aligned text, plenty of space between points.
- A picture next to each point. Suggest one with a note like `[Picture: a calendar with Tuesday circled]`. Don't insert real images unless the user gives them.

## Steps

1. Find the few things the reader must know or do. Usually 3 to 8 points.
2. Put them in the order the reader needs them: what is happening, when, where, what to bring or do, who to ask for help.
3. Write each point as one short sentence, or two at most.
4. Add a `[Picture: ...]` suggestion beside each point.
5. End with who to contact for help, with the phone number or name exactly as given.
6. Check every fact against the original. Nothing important dropped, nothing added.

## Output

The Easy Read version, one point per line with picture suggestions, then a short change summary. For a Word file, return `<name>-easy-read.docx` with large text and space between points, and keep the original untouched.

## Example

Before:

> Patients are kindly requested to report to the outpatient department at least 30 minutes prior to their scheduled appointment on 14 March, bringing valid identification and all relevant previous medical documentation.

After:

> [Picture: a calendar with 14 March circled]
> Your appointment is on 14 March.
>
> [Picture: a clock]
> Come 30 minutes early.
>
> [Picture: a hospital door with a sign]
> Go to the outpatient department.
>
> [Picture: an ID card]
> Bring your ID card.
>
> [Picture: a folder of papers]
> Bring your old medical papers.

What I changed
- Put one idea on each line, with a picture suggestion next to each.
- Swapped "report to" and "prior to" for everyday words.
- Kept the date, the 30 minutes, and every item to bring.
