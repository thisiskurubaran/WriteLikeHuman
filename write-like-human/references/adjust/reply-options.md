# Reply options for tricky messages

Give one good reply, and for sensitive messages, one short offer of a different tone. Never a menu of versions.

## When this applies

- The message is sensitive or tricky: a dispute with a landlord, saying no to a boss, a delicate family message, a payment reminder to a friend, a reply to an angry customer.
- The user seems unsure how strong to be: "I don't want to sound rude", "how do I say this without starting a fight?"
- The user asks for options: "give me two versions", "one softer and one firmer".

It does not apply to ordinary messages. A thank you note or a meeting reminder gets one reply and nothing else.

## What stays the same

- The facts: names, dates, amounts, what was agreed, what went wrong.
- What the user wants to happen. A softer version still asks for the deposit back.
- The user's language and level of formality.

## Steps

1. Work out what the user needs from the reply: money back, a boundary kept, a relationship protected, a problem fixed.
2. Write one reply in the tone that best fits the situation and the user's own words. Lean calm and clear. Calm is usually stronger than angry.
3. After the reply, add one line offering the most likely other direction: "Want it firmer?" or "Want it a bit warmer?" Pick the one the user is most likely to need. Only one offer.
4. If the user asked for options, give exactly two versions, labeled in plain words ("Polite but clear", "Firmer"). Not three.
5. Check both versions keep every fact and the same ask.

## Things to avoid

- Three or more versions. People asked for help deciding, not more choices.
- A "softer" version that drops the actual request.
- A "firmer" version with threats, insults, or legal claims the user didn't make.
- Offering a tone change on a message that is clearly fine.

## Output

The reply, ready to copy, then one line with the tone offer. If two versions were asked for, both versions with short labels, and nothing else.

## Example

Message from the landlord: "I will deduct Rs 8,000 from the deposit for painting."

User: "I lived there 11 months and the flat was painted just before I moved in. Help me reply without a fight."

Reply:

> Hi Mr. Sharma, the flat was freshly painted when I moved in last December, and I've only been here 11 months, so the walls are in the same condition apart from normal wear. Could you share what needs repainting? I'd like the full deposit back unless there's damage beyond that.

Want it firmer? I can mention the rental agreement's clause on normal wear and tear.
