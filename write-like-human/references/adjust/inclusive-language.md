# Inclusive language

An optional pass that swaps wording that leaves people out for wording that includes them. Use only when the user asks.

## When this applies

- "Make it inclusive", "gender neutral", "make sure this doesn't leave anyone out", "is this okay to send to everyone?"
- Job descriptions, school notices, company policies, and public posts where the user asks for this pass.

Do not run this pass unless asked. If a draft has something that could clearly offend its readers, you may mention it in one line, without lecturing.

## What stays the same

- Quotes, names, titles of works, and anything the user is citing.
- Legal terms and wording in contracts, policies, or forms that must match a law or official document.
- How people describe themselves. If a person's bio uses certain words for themselves, keep them.
- The meaning, tone, and length.
- The language. For Hindi, Tamil, and other gendered languages, follow the language file and keep the grammar correct. Do not force English rules onto another language.

## Common swaps

| Instead of | Try |
|---|---|
| chairman | chair, chairperson |
| manpower | staff, people, workforce |
| businessmen | business people, business owners |
| "each employee should submit his form" | "employees should submit their forms" or "each employee should submit their form" |
| "Dear Sir" (unknown reader) | "Dear Sir or Madam", "Hello", or the team name |
| guys (to a mixed group, formal text) | everyone, team, all |
| handicapped, wheelchair-bound | disabled person, person who uses a wheelchair (or follow the reader's preference) |
| normal people (vs disabled) | non-disabled people |
| fair complexion (in listings or ads) | remove, unless it is about something like makeup shade |
| young and dynamic (in job ads) | describe the actual skills needed |

These are suggestions, not rules. Context matters. "Guys" in a WhatsApp to friends is fine.

## Steps

1. Read for wording that assumes one gender, age, ability, religion, caste, body type, or family set up when it does not need to.
2. Swap with the plainest option that keeps the sentence natural. Avoid clunky fixes like "he/she/they" chains.
3. Use singular "they" when the person's gender is unknown or does not matter.
4. Do not add disclaimers, diversity statements, or lectures the user did not ask for.
5. Do not change anything listed in "What stays the same".

## Output

The revised text, then a short change summary listing each swap. Keep the explanation to a few words per swap.

## Example

Before:

> We are looking for a young, dynamic salesman. He should be ready to travel and must have his own bike.

After:

> We are looking for an energetic salesperson. You should be ready to travel and have your own bike.

What I changed
- "Salesman" to "salesperson" and "he" to "you", so the ad speaks to anyone.
- Dropped "young", since age isn't part of the job.
