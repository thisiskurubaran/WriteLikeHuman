# English variants

Switch text between US, UK, Australian, and Indian English: spelling, everyday words, dates, and number formats.

## When this applies

- The user asks: "make it American English", "UK spelling please", "this is for my Australian client", "change it to Indian style".
- The user writes in Singapore, Nigerian, Kenyan, Philippine, or South African English and asks to adapt it for a reader elsewhere, or the reverse. See "More Englishes" below.
- The audience is clearly in another country and the user would want it to fit (a cover letter for a job in London written with US spelling). In that case, offer the switch in one line rather than doing it silently.

Do not switch on your own otherwise. Keep the variety the user writes in. For Indian English style beyond spelling and formats, also read `languages/indian-english.md`.

## What stays the same

- Quotes, names, titles of books and organisations, legal terms, and product names. "Center for Disease Control" stays as written even in UK English.
- Numbers themselves. Only the format changes, and only when you are sure of the value.
- Meaning, tone, and length.
- Text inside code, URLs, email addresses, and file names.

## The main differences

| Area | US | UK | Australia | India |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| -our / -or | color, favor | colour, favour | colour, favour | colour, favour |
| -ise / -ize | organize | organise (or organize) | organise | organise (both seen) |
| -re / -er | center, theater | centre, theatre | centre, theatre | centre, theatre |
| Dates | 07/11/2026 is July 11 | 07/11/2026 is 7 November | 7/11/2026 is 7 November | 07/11/2026 is 7 November |
| Written dates | November 7, 2026 | 7 November 2026 | 7 November 2026 | 7 November 2026 or 7th November 2026 |
| Big numbers | 150,000 and 1.5 million | same as US | same as US | 1,50,000 (1.5 lakh) and 15,00,000 (15 lakh); 1 crore is 10 million |
| Money | $1,500 | £1,500 | $1,500 (AUD if unclear) | ₹1,500 or Rs 1,500 |
| Phone | cell phone | mobile | mobile | mobile |
| Flat | apartment | flat | apartment or unit | flat |
| Holiday | vacation | holiday | holiday | vacation or holiday |
| School years | grade 5 | year 5 | year 5 | class 5 or standard 5 |
| Bill at a restaurant | check | bill | bill | bill |
| Time off from work | PTO, vacation | annual leave | annual leave | leave |

Australian English mostly follows UK spelling, with some US words (truck, program for software). Indian English mostly follows UK spelling and has its own words and formats; see `languages/indian-english.md`.

### More Englishes

These all start from British spelling (colour, centre, organise) and write dates day first (7/11/2026 is 7 November). Each has its own everyday words, and each is correct English.

| Variant | Everyday words and phrases | Numbers and money |
|---|---|---|
| Singapore | "send you home" (drive you home), "on the aircon", "can or not?" in casual chat, "revert" for reply in office email | S$1,500 |
| Nigerian | "well done" as a greeting to someone working, "I'm coming" (I'll be right back), "flash me" (give me a missed call) | ₦150,000 or N150,000 |
| Kenyan | "I'm around" (I'm nearby), "sorry" said in sympathy when something goes wrong, "pole" borrowed from Swahili | KSh 1,500 |
| Philippine | US spelling is common here, unlike the others. "for a while" on the phone (please hold), "comfort room" (toilet), "open the light" in casual speech | ₱1,500 |
| South African | "just now" (later, not immediately), "now now" (soon), "robot" (traffic light), "bakkie" (pickup truck) | R1,500 |

The rules for all of these:

- Keep the user's local English exactly as it is. A Kenyan writing to a Kenyan reader does not need "fixing".
- Switch only when the user asks, or when the reader is clearly elsewhere ("this goes to our office in London"). Even then, change only the words that would confuse that reader. Keep the writer's voice.
- Never make fun of, label as wrong, or "correct" local usage. Never add local slang the user did not use.
- No stereotypes. Talk about what a reader is likely to understand, not about how people from a place are.
- Casual chat forms (like "can or not?") belong in casual messages. In a formal letter, follow the user's lead.

## Steps

1. Confirm the target variety. If the user just says "fix the spelling", keep their own variety.
2. Change spelling consistently across the whole text. Do not mix "colour" and "color".
3. Change everyday words only where the other word would confuse or stand out to the reader. Leave neutral words alone.
4. Convert dates only when the day and month are certain. "03/04/2026" is ambiguous: ask, or write it out ("3 April 2026") only if the user confirms which they meant.
5. Convert number formats carefully. 15,00,000 is 1.5 million, not 15 million. Write the converted figure next to the original in your summary so the user can check it.
6. Do not convert currencies. Changing ₹ to $ changes the amount. Ask if they meant to.
7. Leave protected content (see the core rules) and any protected words list untouched.

## Output

The converted text, then the change summary. Mention the variety and any number or date you reformatted: "Switched to UK spelling. Changed 15,00,000 to 1.5 million and 11/07/2026 to 7 November 2026. Please check these."

## Example

Target: UK English, for a recruiter in Manchester.

Before:

> I organized our company's first offsite for 120 employees and managed a budget of $45,000. I'm available to start on 09/01/2026 and can relocate to an apartment near the office.

After:

> I organised our company's first offsite for 120 employees and managed a budget of $45,000. I'm available to start on 1 September 2026 and can relocate to a flat near the office.

What I changed
- Switched to UK spelling ("organised").
- Wrote the start date out as 1 September 2026, reading 09/01/2026 as US format. Please check that's right.
- Changed "apartment" to "flat".
- Kept the budget in dollars, since that's the amount you managed.
