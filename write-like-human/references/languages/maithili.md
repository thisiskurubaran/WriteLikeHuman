# Maithili

Rewrite Maithili so it sounds like a person wrote it, and keep it Maithili.

## When this applies

- The text is in Maithili, usually in Devanagari, sometimes in Roman script.
- The user asks for a translation into Maithili (also load `translate-humanize.md`).
- For Maithili mixed with Hindi or English, also load `mixed-language.md`.

## What stays the same

- Language. Maithili stays Maithili. Never "correct" Maithili words or verb endings into Hindi. Forms like "छी", "अछि", "हम जाइत छी" are correct Maithili, not mistakes.
- Script. Devanagari stays Devanagari, Roman stays Roman. Never translate unless asked.
- Respect levels. Maithili verbs change with how respectful you are to the listener ("अहाँ" polite, "तोँ" familiar). Keep the level the user chose.
- Names, numbers, dates, amounts, and places.

## AI tells in Maithili

- Hindi grammar or Hindi verb endings slipping in ("मैं जा रहा हूँ" instead of the Maithili form). This is the most common mistake, and it changes the language.
- Sanskritized textbook words in a casual message, where people use everyday words.
- Literal English calques ("I hope you are doing well" word for word).
- Respect levels mixed in one message.
- The same sentence shape repeated.

## Steps

1. Note the respect level and script, and keep both.
2. Remove Hindi forms that crept in and use the Maithili form instead.
3. Swap textbook words for everyday ones in casual writing.
4. Rewrite calques naturally. Vary sentence length.
5. Check names, numbers, and dates are unchanged.

## Output

The rewritten Maithili text, then a short **What I changed** list in the language the user is using with you. Describe the changes in plain words, without word-by-word English glosses of the rewritten text.

## Example

Before: "हम आशा करैत छी जे अहाँ स्वस्थ होएब। काल्हि बैसक दू बजे अछि।"

After: "अहाँ केहन छी? काल्हि बैसक दू बजे अछि।"

Gloss: The first version opens with a stiff, translated "I hope you are healthy. Tomorrow's meeting is at two." The rewrite asks "How are you?" the way people do, keeps the polite अहाँ, and keeps the time exactly.
