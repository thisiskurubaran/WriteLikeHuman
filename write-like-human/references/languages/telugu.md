# Telugu

Rewrite Telugu so it sounds like a person wrote it, in the register the situation needs.

## When this applies

- The text is in Telugu, in Telugu script or in Roman script.
- The user asks for a translation into Telugu (also load `translate-humanize.md`).
- For Telugu mixed with English, load `mixed-language.md` as well.

## What stays the same

- Language and script. Telugu stays Telugu. Never translate unless asked.
- Names, numbers, dates, amounts, and places.
- The register. "మీరు" (respectful) stays "మీరు". "నువ్వు" (informal) stays "నువ్వు".
- Everyday English words people use in Telugu (phone, bus, office), unless the user avoids them.

## AI tells in Telugu

- Bookish (grandhika) style in casual text. Old literary forms in a message to family. Everyday Telugu (vyavaharika) is normal for chats, emails to friends, and most writing.
- Forced pure Telugu words for things people name in English.
- Literal English calques, such as word for word versions of "Please do the needful" or "I hope this finds you well".
- Too formal for the reader. "మీరు" and formal verb endings with a younger sibling.
- Repeated sentence shape, each line ending the same way.

Official letters and school writing can stay formal. Fix stiffness without changing the register.

## Steps

1. Work out the reader and keep the user's register.
2. Replace bookish forms with everyday Telugu in casual text.
3. Rewrite calques the way a Telugu speaker would say them.
4. Vary sentence length.
5. Check names, numbers, and dates are unchanged.

## Output

The rewritten Telugu text, then a short **What I changed** list in the language the user is using with you.

## Example

Before (to a friend): "మిత్రమా, రేపు నేను రాగలనో లేదో నాకు తెలియదు."

After: "రేపు రాగలనో లేదో తెలీదురా."

Gloss: Both mean "I don't know if I can come tomorrow." The first is stiff, opening with "O friend". The second sounds like a real text to a friend.
