# Indian English

Indian English is a full variety of English with its own words, number system, and rhythm. Keep it. Do not turn it into American English.

## When this applies

- The writer is in India or writes for Indian readers.
- The text uses lakh, crore, ₹, DD/MM/YYYY dates, or Indian phrasing.
- The user asks for something "for my Indian office", "for my bank", or similar.

## What stays the same

- Lakh and crore. "₹4.5 lakh" stays "₹4.5 lakh". Do not convert to "450,000" or "$5,400".
- Rupee formatting. Keep Indian digit grouping: ₹1,50,000, not ₹150,000. Keep "Rs." or "INR" if that is what the user wrote.
- Dates. DD/MM/YYYY or "7 November 2026". Never swap to MM/DD.
- Spelling. British spelling by default: colour, organise, cheque, programme, centre. Keep American spelling only if the user already uses it.
- Indian words people actually use. "Prepone", "do the needful", "kindly", "revert", "out of station", "passed out" (graduated), "cousin brother". Keep them where the user already writes that way. Do not add them where the user did not.
- Names, titles, and honorifics like "Sir", "Ma'am", "ji".

## AI tells in Indian English writing

- Forced American style. "Gotten", "zip code", "vacation" in a note to an Indian HR team, or converting amounts to dollars.
- Stock corporate filler. "I hope this email finds you well", "Please do not hesitate to reach out". Real Indian office mail is often shorter: "Hi Ramesh, sharing the file. Let me know if anything is needed."
- Over-polished grammar that kills the voice. Rewriting "Kindly share the details at the earliest" into "Could you please send me the details as soon as possible at your earliest convenience?" Both are fine English. The first is the user's voice.
- Oddly cold tone. Indian workplace writing is often warmer than American corporate style. "Hope you and family are doing well" in a long-standing relationship is normal.

Do not "fix" Indian English into "standard" English. Only fix what is actually unclear or robotic.

## Steps

1. Check whether the user writes in Indian English. If so, keep its features.
2. Keep every number in lakh and crore, and every date in the user's format.
3. Fix stiff or AI-sounding lines, keeping the user's words where possible.
4. Use British spelling unless the user writes American.

## Output

The rewritten text and a short **What I changed** list. If you kept a phrase on purpose, there is no need to mention it.

## Example

Before: "I hope this email finds you well. I am writing to inform you that the payment of $1,800 (₹150,000) has been successfully processed on 11/07/2026. Please do not hesitate to reach out if you have any questions."

After: "Hi Ramesh, the payment of ₹1,50,000 went through on 07/11/2026. Let me know if you need anything else."

Note: if the original date was really 7 November, keep it as 07/11/2026. If you cannot tell which format the user meant, ask rather than guess.
