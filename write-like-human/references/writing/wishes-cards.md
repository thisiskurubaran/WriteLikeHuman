# Wishes and cards

Festival greetings, birthday and anniversary wishes, congratulations, get well messages, and condolences. Short messages where sounding sincere matters more than sounding polished.

## When this applies

- The user wants a greeting for a festival: Diwali, Eid, Holi, Pongal, Onam, Durga Puja, Christmas, Lunar New Year, Navroz, Vaisakhi, and others.
- A birthday, anniversary, wedding, new baby, new job, exam result, or house warming.
- A card, a WhatsApp wish, a note in a gift, or a message to a group.
- A condolence or sympathy message.

## What stays the same

- Names, relationships, and the event.
- The festival name as the user spells it.
- Any religious or cultural words the user chose ("Eid Mubarak", "Iniya Pongal Nalvazhthukkal").
- The language. A wish in Tamil stays in Tamil unless they ask to translate.

## Tone by occasion

Festivals and celebrations: warm and simple. One personal line beats three generic ones. "Hope the kids burst fewer crackers than last year" is better than "May this Diwali bring prosperity, joy, and light".

Congratulations: name what they did. "Congrats on clearing CA finals" is better than "Congrats on your amazing achievement".

Get well: keep it light and practical. Offer something real if the user wants to ("I can drop food on Thursday").

Condolences: see below. This needs the most care.

## Condolences

- Keep it short. Three or four sentences is plenty.
- Say the person's name if the user gives it.
- Do not say "everything happens for a reason", "they're in a better place", or "at least...". Do not try to find a bright side.
- No jokes. No emoji unless the user uses them and knows the family uses them.
- A shared memory helps, but only one the user gives you.
- Offer help only if the user means it, and make it specific.
- Respect the family's tradition. "Om Shanti", "Inna lillahi wa inna ilayhi raji'un", "Rest in peace", or "May their memory be a blessing" depend on the family. Ask if unsure, or leave the line out.
- For a message to a manager or colleague, keep it plain and brief. "I'm so sorry about your father. Please take all the time you need."

## Steps

1. Check the occasion, the relationship, and how they will send it (card, WhatsApp, email, group).
2. Ask for one personal detail if there is none. If they want it fast, write a short simple version without one.
3. Cut stacked wishes ("joy, peace, prosperity, health, and happiness"). Keep one or two.
4. Match length to the channel. A WhatsApp wish is one to three lines. A card can be a short paragraph.
5. For groups (family group, office group), keep it short and general.

## Output

The message, ready to copy. If it helps, give a second shorter version. Then a brief change summary.

## Example

Before:

> Wishing you and your family a very Happy Diwali! May this festival of lights illuminate your life with endless joy, prosperity, good health, and success in all your endeavours.

After:

> Happy Diwali, Sunita aunty! Hope the house is full and the sweets last longer than one day this time. Love to everyone at home.

Before (condolence):

> I am deeply saddened to hear about your loss. Your grandfather has gone to a better place and is watching over you. Stay strong and positive!

After:

> I'm so sorry about your grandfather, Imran. I know how close you two were. Thinking of you and your family.
