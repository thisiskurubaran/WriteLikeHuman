# Negotiation messages

Help people ask for a better deal in plain, polite, specific words: a higher salary offer, a better price from a vendor, a fair rate from a client, or lower rent.

## When this applies

- Salary and job offer negotiation: base pay, joining bonus, notice buyout, start date, remote days.
- Price talks with vendors, suppliers, contractors, or wedding and event services.
- Freelancers and small businesses setting or defending their rates with a client.
- Rent and deposit discussions with a landlord or a tenant.
- The user pastes a draft that sounds either too timid ("sorry to bother, if possible...") or too aggressive.

## What stays the same

- Every number exactly as the user gave it: the offer, the counter, the budget, the market rate they found, dates, percentages.
- The user's real reasons. Years of experience, a specific skill, a quote from another vendor, a long rental history, only if the user told you.
- What the user is willing to accept. Their fallback is theirs to set.

## What you never do

- Invent a competing offer, a quote from another vendor, a "my friend pays less", or any bluff. If the user has a real one, use it accurately.
- Invent market data or salary figures. If the user wants to mention a market rate, they give the number and where it came from.
- Make threats or ultimatums the user didn't ask for.
- Promise something on the user's behalf ("I can join tomorrow") that they didn't say.

## Steps

1. Find the one main ask. "Can the base be Rs 18 lakh instead of Rs 16 lakh?" Lead with thanks or the shared goal in one line, then the ask.
2. Give the reason in one or two sentences, using only the user's facts.
3. Be specific. A clear number or term ("Rs 4,500 per day", "a 2,000 rupee lower rent", "start on 1 February") is easier to say yes to than "a bit more".
4. Add a fallback only if the user has one and approves it: "If the base can't move, would a Rs 1.5 lakh joining bonus work?" Ask before including it.
5. Keep the tone warm and firm. No over-apologising, no flattery, no pressure.
6. End with an easy next step: "Happy to discuss on a call this week."
7. Check every number against what the user wrote.

## Output

The message, ready to send, followed by a short change summary. If the user didn't say what they'd accept as a fallback, add one line asking whether they want to offer one. Keep salary and rent messages short, usually under 150 words.

## Example

Before (user's draft):

> Hi, thank you so much for the offer!! I am very excited. I was wondering if maybe there is any possibility at all to increase the salary a little bit if it's not too much trouble, because I feel I deserve more. Sorry if this is rude.

User's facts: offer is Rs 16 lakh, they want Rs 18 lakh, they have 5 years in payments testing and led the UPI migration at their current job. Fallback: a joining bonus.

After:

> Hi Ritu, thank you for the offer. I'm excited about the role and the team.
>
> Before I accept, I'd like to ask whether the base salary can be Rs 18 lakh. I've spent five years in payments testing and led our UPI migration last year, which lines up closely with what this role needs.
>
> If the base can't move, would a joining bonus be possible instead? Happy to talk it through on a call.
>
> Thanks, Arjun

What I changed
- Put the exact number in, instead of "a little bit".
- Used your real reasons: five years in payments testing and the UPI migration.
- Removed the apology and the double exclamation marks, which made the ask sound unsure.
- Added the joining bonus fallback you mentioned.
