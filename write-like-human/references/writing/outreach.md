# Cold messages and outreach

Help someone write a first message to a person they don't know yet: a LinkedIn connection note, a referral request, a cold email to a recruiter, or a pitch to a possible client.

These messages get ignored when they are long, vague, or sound copied. They get answered when they are short, specific, and ask for one thing.

## When this applies

- "Write a LinkedIn connection note to this recruiter."
- "Help me ask my senior from college for a referral."
- "Cold email to a hiring manager", "pitch my design services to this café".
- The user pastes an outreach draft that sounds like a template.

## What stays the same

- The user's real reason for writing and their real background: role, years of experience, skills, projects, links.
- Names, company names, job titles, job IDs, and any link the user gives.
- The ask. If they want a referral, the message asks for a referral, not a "quick chat" that hides it.

Never invent a mutual connection, a shared college, a past meeting, a compliment about a post the user never read, or a result the user never got. If the message would be better with a real link between them ("we were both at the Pune design meetup"), ask whether there is one.

## Lengths

- LinkedIn connection note: about 300 characters. The exact limit changes and can depend on the account, so keep it well under and suggest the user check the box count.
- LinkedIn message or InMail: three to six short lines.
- Cold email: under about 120 words. A subject line that says what it is: "Referral for the data analyst role (JR-4471)".
- Client pitch: under about 150 words, with one example of their work as a link.

## Steps

1. Find the one ask. Referral, a 15 minute call, feedback on a portfolio, or a reply about an open role. One ask per message.
2. Open with who they are in one line, then why this person. Use a real reason the user gave: the role posting, a shared employer, an article they actually read.
3. Give one or two specific facts that make saying yes easy: "3 years in supply chain analytics, mostly SQL and Power BI". Not a list of every skill.
4. Make the ask clear and easy to act on: "Would you be open to referring me? I've attached my resume and the job ID."
5. Close politely with no pressure. Skip "Looking forward to hearing from you at your earliest convenience" and similar.
6. Cut flattery that reads as fake ("I've long admired your incredible journey"). Cut "I hope you're doing well" in short notes.
7. Check every name, company, and ID against what the user gave.

## Output

The message, ready to paste, with the subject line for emails. For connection notes, add the approximate character count. Then a short **What I changed** list.

## Example

Before (LinkedIn note):

> Hi Ananya, I hope this message finds you well! I came across your inspiring profile and was truly impressed by your remarkable journey. I am a passionate and results-driven professional and would love to connect and explore potential synergies. Looking forward to connecting!

After:

> Hi Ananya, I saw the product analyst opening on your team at Quillpay. I've spent 2 years in analytics at a lending startup, mostly SQL and A/B tests. Would you be open to a short chat about the role?

(About 200 characters.)

What I changed
- Dropped the general praise and said why you're writing to her: the opening on her team.
- Swapped "passionate and results-driven" for what you actually do.
- Ended with one clear ask.
