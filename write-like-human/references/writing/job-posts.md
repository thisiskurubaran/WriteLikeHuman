# Job posts and job descriptions

Help people write hiring posts that say plainly what the job is, so the right people apply.

## When this applies

- A small business, startup, school, clinic, or household is hiring and has a draft post.
- Someone wants a job description, a LinkedIn hiring post, a Naukri or Indeed listing, or a WhatsApp group post.
- The draft is full of buzzwords ("rockstar", "ninja", "fast-paced environment", "self-starter").

Also read `lists/buzzwords.md` for plain replacements. For wording that might put people off for no reason (gendered words, age hints), use the checks in `adjust/inclusive-language.md`. For a LinkedIn post, also load `writing/social-post.md`.

## What stays the same

- Job title, location, work mode (office, hybrid, remote), and working hours.
- Salary or stipend, and whether it is per month or per year. Keep the exact figure or range.
- Requirements the employer actually needs: qualifications, years of experience, languages, licences.
- Benefits the employer offers. Only these.
- How to apply, the contact, and the deadline.
- Legal or policy lines (equal opportunity statements, background checks).

## Never invent

- Benefits, perks, or culture claims: health insurance, flexible hours, "great team culture", growth paths.
- Salary figures, if the employer did not give one. Leave `[salary range]` and mention that posts with a salary tend to get more relevant applicants.
- Requirements the employer did not ask for.

## Steps

1. Pull out the facts: role, location, mode, pay, hours, what the person will do, what they need, how to apply.
2. Open with the role and the key facts in one or two lines. People scan job posts quickly.
3. Describe the work in plain, specific lines: what a normal week looks like. "Answer customer calls and WhatsApp messages, and update orders in our sheet" beats "Drive customer delight across touchpoints".
4. Split requirements into must-have and nice-to-have if the employer gave both. Long must-have lists put good people off.
5. Swap buzzwords using `lists/buzzwords.md`. Replace each one with the plain word it stands for ("rockstar ninja developer" becomes "developer"). Use a `[bracket]` only for a fact that is missing, like the salary, never for a word you took out.
6. Run the inclusive checks: use "you" and "they", drop "young and energetic", drop "he will", keep real requirements.
7. End with how to apply, clearly.
8. Check every fact against the draft.

## Output

The post, ready to paste, then a short change summary. List any blanks, like salary or deadline, that would help the post.

## Example

Before:

> We are looking for a young, dynamic rockstar to join our fast-paced family! He should be a self-starter who thrives in ambiguity and can wear many hats. Excellent communication skills a must. Competitive salary. Interested candidates DM.

Employer's notes: "front desk at our dental clinic in Anna Nagar, 9 to 6 Mon to Sat, Rs 18,000 to 22,000 a month, Tamil and English, handle appointments and billing"

After:

> Front desk role at our dental clinic, Anna Nagar, Chennai
>
> Monday to Saturday, 9 am to 6 pm. Rs 18,000 to 22,000 a month.
>
> You'll greet patients, book and confirm appointments, and handle billing at the counter. You need to speak Tamil and English comfortably.
>
> To apply, send us a message with a short note about yourself.

What I changed
- Put the role, location, hours, and pay at the top.
- Replaced "rockstar", "thrives in ambiguity", and "wear many hats" with the actual tasks.
- Removed "young" and "he", which could put off good applicants.
- Kept the salary range and hours exactly as you gave them.
