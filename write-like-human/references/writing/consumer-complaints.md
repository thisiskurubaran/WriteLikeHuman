# Consumer complaints

Help someone complain to a company: an online shop, an airline, a telecom provider, a bank, a delivery app, or an insurance company. The aim is to get the problem fixed, so the message must be clear, calm, and easy to act on.

For complaint letters to a government office, a school, or a housing society, use `writing/formal-letters.md` instead or as well.

## When this applies

- "Write a complaint to Flipkart, my refund hasn't come."
- "My flight was cancelled and the airline won't refund."
- "Help me escalate this to the bank."
- The user pastes an angry draft and wants it to work better.

## What stays the same

- Every reference exactly as given: order ID, booking reference (PNR), ticket or complaint number, account or policy number (or only the last four digits, see below), transaction ID, amounts, dates, and times.
- What happened and what the user wants: refund, replacement, repair, compensation, or an explanation.
- Names of staff or earlier responses the user quotes.

Do not invent dates, earlier calls, promises the company made, or rules the company supposedly broke. If the user is not sure of a date, keep "around 12 March".

Privacy: in emails and web forms, suggest giving only the last four digits of card and account numbers, and never a PIN, OTP, CVV, or password. Say so if the user's draft includes one.

## Steps

1. Put the key facts at the top: what was bought or booked, the reference number, the date, and the amount.
2. Say what went wrong in two or three plain sentences, in time order.
3. Mention what the user already tried, with dates and complaint numbers: "I called customer care on 14 March (complaint no. 88213) and was told to wait 7 days."
4. State the exact ask and a reasonable deadline if the user wants one: "Please refund ₹2,349 to my original payment method within 7 days."
5. Keep the tone firm and calm. Remove insults, capitals, and threats the user cannot back up. Keep real consequences the user plans, like escalating.
6. List attachments the user has: invoice, screenshots, photos of the damaged item.
7. Check every number against the original.

## Escalation, in plain words

If the first complaint gets nowhere, the usual path in India is below. Rules, numbers, and portals change, so always tell the user to check the current details on the official site before relying on them.

1. Customer care: the company's app, email, or helpline. Keep the complaint number.
2. Grievance or nodal officer: most companies, banks, telecoms, and airlines must name one on their website. Write again, quoting the first complaint number.
3. Outside help:
   - Shopping and most services: the National Consumer Helpline (1915, or the NCH app and website), and for formal cases the e-Daakhil consumer commission portal.
   - Banks, cards, and digital payments: the RBI Ombudsman through the RBI Complaint Management System (CMS) portal, usually after the bank has had time to reply.
   - Airlines and telecom have their own regulator routes. Point the user to the official site rather than guessing.

Present this as options, not legal advice. Do not promise an outcome.

## Output

The complaint, ready to send, with a clear subject line such as "Refund not received, order OD4421987, ₹2,349". Then a short **What I changed** list, and the next escalation step in one line if the user asked.

## Example

Before:

> THIS IS THE WORST SERVICE EVER!!! I ordered a mixer and it came broken and NOBODY is helping me. I want my money back NOW or I will go to the court. You people are cheaters.

After:

> Subject: Damaged item, refund requested, order OD4421987
>
> Hello, I ordered a Kitchenmate 750W mixer grinder (order OD4421987) for ₹2,349, delivered on 9 March. The large jar arrived cracked. I raised a return request on 9 March (request no. RT55120), but it was closed on 12 March without a pickup.
>
> Please arrange a pickup and refund the full amount to my original payment method within 7 days. Photos of the cracked jar and the box are attached.
>
> Thank you, Ritu Menon

What I changed
- Put the order number, amount, and dates at the top so the agent can act quickly.
- Kept the anger out and the facts in, which usually gets a faster reply.
- Left out the court threat. If nothing happens in 7 days, the next step is the National Consumer Helpline (check the current number and portal first).

(The order details, dates, and request number were in the user's notes in this example. Without them, ask for the order ID and dates instead of filling them in.)
