# RTI applications

Help someone write a Right to Information (RTI) application in India: a request to a government office for information it holds, under the Right to Information Act, 2005.

An RTI works best when it is short, polite, and asks precise questions that the office can answer from its records. It is not a complaint letter. If the user wants to complain, use `writing/formal-letters.md` or `writing/consumer-complaints.md`.

## When this applies

- "Help me file an RTI about my pending passport, pension, scholarship, or road work."
- The user pastes a draft RTI and asks to make it proper or clearer.
- The user wants to know what happened to an application or complaint they made to a public office.

## What stays the same

- The legal wording of the opening: "under Section 6(1) of the Right to Information Act, 2005".
- The office, department, and address the user gives. The application goes to the Public Information Officer (PIO) of that office.
- All facts the user gives: application numbers, file numbers, dates, ward or village names, amounts, and their own name and address.
- Fee details: the user's actual payment method (postal order, demand draft, court fee stamp, or online payment) and its number. Fees and payment methods differ between the central government and each state, so tell the user to check the current fee for that office.

Never invent facts, file numbers, dates, or names of officials. If something is missing, leave a blank in square brackets.

## Rules for the questions

- Ask for information, records, or documents, not opinions or explanations. "Please provide the date on which my application no. 4471 was received and its current status" works. "Why are you so slow?" does not.
- Number each question. Keep each to one thing.
- Ask for certified copies when the user needs documents: "certified copies of the file notings on application no. 4471".
- Give a time period where it helps: "between 1 April 2024 and 31 March 2025".
- Keep it to a few questions. Long RTIs get delayed or split.
- No accusations, insults, or requests for action. An RTI asks what the records say.

## Format

```
To
The Public Information Officer
[Office name]
[Address]

Subject: Application under Section 6(1) of the Right to Information Act, 2005

Sir/Madam,

I request the following information under Section 6(1) of the Right to Information Act, 2005:

1. ...
2. ...

Period: [if relevant]

I am paying the application fee of Rs [amount] by [method, number].
[Or: I belong to the Below Poverty Line category. A copy of my BPL card is attached, so no fee is payable.]

I am a citizen of India. Please send the information by post to the address below [or by email to ...].

Place:
Date:

Name:
Address:
Phone (optional):
```

Keep the "I am a citizen of India" line. It is part of the usual format.

## Steps

1. Find what the user really wants to know. Turn their worry into specific questions the records can answer.
2. Check the office and PIO address. If the user is unsure, leave it as a blank and say where to look (the department's website usually lists its PIO).
3. Write the questions as numbered requests for information.
4. Add the fee line with the user's payment details, or the BPL line if it applies and they have the card.
5. Remove complaints and emotional lines from the body. Offer to write a separate complaint if they want one.
6. Check every number and date against the user's notes.

## Output

The RTI application, ready to print or paste into the online RTI portal, then a short **What I changed** list. Add one line reminding the user to check the current fee and to keep a copy and the postal receipt. The usual reply period is 30 days, but tell them to confirm on the official site.

## Example

Before:

> I applied for my widow pension 8 months ago and nobody is doing anything. The officers are not bothered. I want to know why this delay and who is responsible and when I will get my money.

After (questions only):

> 1. Please provide the current status of my widow pension application no. WP/2025/1187, submitted on 14 March 2025.
> 2. Please provide the dates on which the application was received and moved from one officer or section to another, with the names of those sections.
> 3. Please provide certified copies of the file notings on this application.
> 4. Please state the time limit, as per the department's rules or citizen's charter, for deciding such applications.

What I changed
- Turned "why this delay" into questions the office must answer from its records.
- Kept your application number and date exactly.
- Left out the lines about officers, since an RTI asks for information, not blame. I can write a separate complaint if you want.
