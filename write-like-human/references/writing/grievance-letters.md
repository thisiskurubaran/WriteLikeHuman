# Grievance letters to an employer

Complaints to a manager or HR about pay, leave, working conditions, or harassment, written calmly and factually.

## When this applies

- "Help me complain to HR about my unpaid salary", "write to my manager about leave being refused".
- Complaints about workload, overtime, deductions, unfair treatment, or harassment.
- An angry draft the user wants to make professional without losing the point.

## What stays the same

- Every fact: dates, amounts, names, what was said, what happened. Exactly as the user tells it.
- The seriousness of the complaint. Calm does not mean softer. A harassment complaint stays a harassment complaint.
- Never add events, witnesses, or quotes the user didn't give. Never exaggerate. An exaggerated detail can weaken a true complaint.

## Steps

1. Ask for the facts if they're missing: what happened, when (dates), who was involved, what the user has already done, and what outcome they want.
2. Open with the purpose in one line: "I am writing to raise a formal complaint about my salary for September and October 2026, which has not been paid."
3. List the events in date order, one line each, plainly. No adjectives doing the arguing.
4. State the outcome the user wants: payment by a date, a meeting, the leave approved, an inquiry.
5. Close politely and firmly, and ask for a written reply.
6. Remind the user, in one or two lines after the letter:
   - Keep a copy of the letter and any replies. Send it in writing (email is fine) so there's a record.
   - For sexual harassment at work in India, the complaint can go to the company's Internal Committee (ICC) under the POSH Act. If the company has no committee, there is a Local Committee at the district level. Suggest checking the company policy.
   - For serious or legal matters, suggest speaking to a lawyer or a labour office.

## Things to avoid

- Insults, threats, sarcasm, or guesses about motives ("he clearly hates me").
- Legal claims the user didn't make.
- Making a complaint sound vague to seem polite.

## Output

The letter, ready to send, then the short reminders above. Leave blanks for missing facts: `[date of the meeting]`.

## Example

Before:

> I am totally fed up!! My salary has not come for 2 months and nobody in HR even bothers to reply. This is completely unacceptable and I will take action.

After:

> Subject: Unpaid salary for September and October 2026
>
> Dear Ms. Nair,
>
> I am writing to raise a formal complaint about my salary for September and October 2026, which has not been paid. I emailed HR about this on 3 October and 20 October and have not received a reply.
>
> Please confirm in writing by 15 November 2026 when the payment will be made.
>
> Regards,
> Vikram Iyer, Accounts team

What I changed
- Kept the two months and your two emails, with their dates.
- Replaced the anger with a clear request and a deadline.
- Dropped "I will take action". It's vague, and the deadline makes the point.
