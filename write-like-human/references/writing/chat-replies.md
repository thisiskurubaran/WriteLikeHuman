# WhatsApp and SMS replies

## When this applies

The user pastes a WhatsApp, SMS, or other chat message and wants a reply, or wants their own message to sound less stiff. Typical senders: family, the landlord, the society or building group, a delivery person, a plumber, a school parents group, a colleague on chat.

## What stays the same

- Times, dates, amounts, flat numbers, addresses, OTP-free details like order IDs.
- What the user agreed or refused. If they said they will pay rent on the 5th, the reply still says the 5th.
- The language mix. If the user writes in Hinglish or Tanglish, reply in it (see `languages/mixed-language.md`). If they write in plain English, stay in English.
- Their way of addressing people: "Uncle", "Aunty", "Bhaiya", "Anna", "Sir". Keep these, they matter.

## How chat is different

A chat message is not a letter. People text in short lines, skip greetings in the middle of a thread, and get to the point. A reply that opens with "Dear Sir, I hope you are doing well" on WhatsApp sounds like a bot.

- One to three short lines is normal. Longer only if the user needs to explain something.
- No subject line, no sign-off, no "Regards" unless the user always signs off that way.
- Contractions are fine. "I'll", "can't", "won't".
- Emoji only if the user uses them, and no more than they do.
- Full stops at the end of every line can read as cold to some people. Follow the user's habit.

## Steps

1. Work out who the reply is to and what it needs to do: confirm, say no, ask, chase, thank, or calm things down.
2. Match the register. Family and friends: casual. Landlord, school, society secretary: polite but still short. Delivery person: clear and practical.
3. Lead with the answer. "Yes, 6 pm works" before any explanation.
4. Cut padding like "I wanted to let you know that" and "Please be informed that".
5. For anything tense (rent dispute, noise complaint, a pushy relative), stay calm and plain. No sarcasm, no passive aggression, no essays.
6. Check the length again. If it would need scrolling on a phone, cut it.
7. Write the change summary, kept very short.

## Common situations

**Landlord.** Polite, clear about dates and amounts, nothing that could be read as agreeing to something the user did not agree to. "Rent will be transferred on 5 Aug as usual. The geyser is still not working, can someone check it this week?"

**Delivery person.** Practical directions only. "Flat 302, B wing. Leave it with the security guard if I don't pick up."

**Family group.** Warm, short, no lecture. If the user is saying no to an event, give the reason they gave and one warm line.

**Society or building group.** Neutral and polite, since many people read it. No naming and shaming unless the user insists.

**Saying no.** Say it early. "Sorry Aunty, can't make it on Sunday, we're travelling. Will come by next week."

## Output

The reply, ready to paste, with no quotes around it. If a second option would really help (one firmer, one softer), give two and label them in plain words. Then a two or three bullet change summary.

## Example

Before:

```
Dear Sir, I hope you are doing well. I would like to inform you that I will
not be able to be present at home tomorrow between the hours of 10 AM and
1 PM. Kindly arrange for the plumber to visit at an alternative time that is
convenient. Thanking you in advance for your kind cooperation.
```

After:

```
Hi Sir, I won't be home tomorrow from 10 to 1. Can the plumber come after
2 instead? Thanks.
```

What I changed
- Cut it down to what a WhatsApp message usually looks like.
- Asked for a specific new time, since you said after 2 works.
- Kept the times exactly as you had them.

If the user had not mentioned a new time, the reply would ask "Can the plumber come at another time?" and leave it to the landlord.
