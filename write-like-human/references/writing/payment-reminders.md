# Payment reminders and invoice notes

Help freelancers, tutors, small shops, and service providers ask to be paid without sounding rude or desperate. Covers invoice cover notes and the follow-ups that come after.

## When this applies

- A freelancer, shop, tutor, or contractor wants to send an invoice with a short note.
- A payment is late and they want a first reminder, a second reminder, or a final notice.
- They paste a draft that sounds angry, pleading, or stiff ("Kindly do the needful and release the payment at the earliest").

## What stays the same

- Every amount, exactly as written: Rs 18,500 stays Rs 18,500, not "around 18k".
- Invoice numbers, order numbers, and due dates.
- Payment details: UPI ID, bank name, account number, IFSC, payment link. Copy them character for character. Never "fix" what looks like a typo in an ID or account number. If something looks wrong, ask.
- The user's terms. Mention a late fee, interest, or a pause in work only if the user's own terms or message include it.
- The relationship. A regular client gets a warmer note than a stranger who has gone silent.

## The three stages

Use the stage the user asks for. If they don't say, ask how late the payment is, or pick from the dates they gave.

1. Invoice note or first reminder: friendly and short. Assume it slipped their mind. One line of context, the amount, the due date, and how to pay.
2. Second reminder: still polite, more direct. Mention the earlier note and the date it was due. Ask for a payment date if they can't pay now.
3. Final notice: calm and firm. State what is owed and since when. Say what happens next only if the user decided it (work paused, late fee per the agreement). No threats, no shaming, no legal language the user didn't give you.

## Steps

1. Collect the facts the user gave: who, what for, amount, invoice number, due date, how to pay. Leave clear blanks like `[invoice number]` for anything missing.
2. Pick the stage and the channel. WhatsApp reminders are two or three lines. Emails get a subject line with the invoice number, like "Invoice INV-204, due 5 March".
3. Lead with the point. "Just a reminder that invoice INV-204 for Rs 18,500 was due on 5 March" beats two lines of pleasantries.
4. Make paying easy. Put the payment details on their own lines so they can be copied.
5. Keep the tone even. No guilt ("I have bills to pay too"), no sarcasm, no capital letters for emphasis.
6. Offer a way out if they're stuck: "If you need to pay in two parts, let me know." Only if the user is open to it.
7. Check every number and ID against what the user gave you before showing the draft.

## Output

The message ready to send, then a short note on which stage it is and anything left blank. For email, include the subject line.

## Example

User: "client hasn't paid invoice INV-204, Rs 18,500, due 5 March, its 20 March now, second reminder, UPI is meera.design@okaxis"

Draft:

> Subject: Invoice INV-204, due 5 March
>
> Hi Arjun,
>
> Following up on invoice INV-204 for Rs 18,500, which was due on 5 March. I haven't seen the payment come through yet.
>
> You can pay by UPI to:
> meera.design@okaxis
>
> If it's already on its way, thank you, and please ignore this. If you need a few more days, just tell me a date that works.
>
> Thanks,
> Meera

What I kept: the invoice number, amount, due date, and UPI ID exactly as given. I didn't mention a late fee because you didn't say your terms include one.
