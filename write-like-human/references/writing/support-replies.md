# Customer support replies

Help small businesses answer customer messages: WhatsApp Business chats, support emails, and buyer messages on Amazon, Flipkart, Meesho, and similar marketplaces.

This is different from replies to public reviews (see `writing/review-replies.md`) and from a customer writing their own complaint (see `writing/consumer-complaints.md`). Here the user is the business, replying privately to one customer.

## When this applies

- A seller, shop, clinic, tutor, or service business wants to answer a customer's question or complaint.
- They paste a draft reply that sounds robotic ("We regret the inconvenience caused") or defensive.
- They paste the customer's message and ask what to say.

## What stays the same

- Order IDs, ticket numbers, tracking numbers, and product names, exactly as given.
- What the business will actually do. Refunds, replacements, discounts, and delivery dates go in only if the user said they will offer them.
- Real timelines the user gives ("refund in 5 to 7 working days"). Never make one up.
- The business's policy, as the user states it. Don't soften a "no" into a maybe.

## Privacy

- Don't repeat the customer's phone number, address, or payment details back to them unless the reply needs it.
- Use the customer's first name if the user gave it. Don't add personal details from their order history unless they help.

## Steps

1. Find the customer's actual problem in one line: late parcel, wrong size, refund not received, question about use.
2. Acknowledge it specifically. "Sorry your order arrived with a cracked lid" beats "Sorry for the inconvenience".
3. Apologise once, and only if the business got something wrong. If it was the courier or the customer, be kind without taking blame the user doesn't accept.
4. Say what happens next, using only what the user told you: "We've sent a replacement. It should reach you by Friday."
5. If the user hasn't decided yet, say what you'll check and when you'll come back, and leave a blank like `[when you'll reply]`.
6. Make the next step easy for the customer: what to send (a photo, the order ID) and where.
7. Match the channel. WhatsApp: two to four short lines. Email: a short greeting, the answer, a sign-off with the business name.
8. Vary wording if the user answers many messages. Customers notice copy-pasted replies.

## Output

The reply ready to send, then one line listing anything left blank for the user to decide (a refund, a date).

## Example

Customer: "Order #MSH-55821 came today but the bottle lid is cracked and it leaked. Very disappointed."

Before (the user's draft):

> Dear Customer, we regret the inconvenience caused. Your concern has been noted and will be addressed at the earliest.

After:

> Hi Priya, sorry about that. A cracked lid is our packing mistake. Could you send a photo of the bottle and the box here? Once I see it, I'll send a replacement for order #MSH-55821 the same day.

What I changed
- Named the actual problem instead of "the inconvenience".
- Took responsibility once, since you said it was your packing.
- Said exactly what you'll do, using the replacement you offered.
- Kept the order ID as it was.
