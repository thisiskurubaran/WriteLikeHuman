# Invitations

Help people invite friends, family, and colleagues to weddings, housewarmings (griha pravesh), naming ceremonies, birthday parties, anniversaries, and get-togethers.

## When this applies

- The user wants text for a printed card, an e-invite, or a WhatsApp invite.
- They paste an invite that sounds stiff, generic, or copied from a template.
- They need the same invite in two styles, like a formal card for elders and a casual WhatsApp message for friends.

For festival greetings, congratulations, and condolences, use `writing/wishes-cards.md` instead.

## What stays the same

These must be exact. Check each one against what the user gave you:

- Names of the hosts, the couple, the child, or the birthday person, with spellings as given.
- Date, day, and time. If the date and the day do not match (14 March is not a Sunday that year), point it out instead of guessing which one is right.
- Venue name and address, and any map link.
- RSVP name, phone number, and deadline.
- Dress code, gift notes ("no gifts please"), and food notes, if given.
- Religious and family phrasing the user includes, like "With the blessings of", names of grandparents, or a shloka or verse. Do not drop or rewrite these.

## Cultural care

- Formal Indian wedding cards often follow a set order: blessings, the families inviting, the couple, the ceremonies with times, the venue, RSVP. Keep that order if the user's draft uses it.
- Do not add religious lines, gods' names, or customs the user did not include. Families differ.
- Ceremony names stay as the family writes them: Mehendi, Haldi, Sangeet, Nikah, Walima, Muhurtham, Griha Pravesh, Namakaran, Naamkaran.
- For multi-faith or inter-community events, keep both families' phrasing if given.

## Steps

1. Pull out the facts: occasion, who is inviting, who it is for, date, time, venue, RSVP.
2. Pick the style the user needs: printed card (formal and complete), WhatsApp (shorter, warmer), or both.
3. Open with the occasion and the invitation, not a long preamble.
4. List event details on separate lines so they are easy to find on a phone.
5. For WhatsApp, keep it to five to eight lines. People will forward it, so it must make sense on its own.
6. Keep warmth simple. "We'd love to have you there" is enough. Skip lines like "Your gracious presence will make our joyous occasion truly memorable".
7. Check every fact again.

## Output

The invite text, ready to copy. If the user asked for both a card and a WhatsApp version, give both, card first. Then a short change summary. List any blanks still to fill, like the RSVP number.

## Example

Before (WhatsApp):

> Dear All, with immense joy and happiness we cordially invite you and your esteemed family to grace the auspicious occasion of the Griha Pravesh of our new abode. Your gracious presence will make this joyous celebration truly memorable!! Date 9th Feb Sunday, time 10 am, Flat 1203 Lakeview Residency, Hadapsar.

After:

> Hi all, we've moved into our new home and we're having a small griha pravesh puja. We'd love for you and your family to come.
>
> Sunday, 9 Feb, 10 am
> Flat 1203, Lakeview Residency, Hadapsar
>
> Lunch after the puja. Please let us know if you can make it.
> Sunita and Prakash

What I changed
- Swapped the long formal opening for a plain invitation.
- Put the date, time, and address on their own lines so they are easy to find.
- Kept the date, time, and address exactly as you had them.

(The lunch line and the names are shown as if the user provided them. In real use, only include what they told you, and leave `[your names]` as a blank otherwise.)
