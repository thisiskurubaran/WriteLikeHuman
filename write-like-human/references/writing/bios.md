# Bios and About pages

## When this applies

Instagram and other social bios, dating app profiles, a website About page for a person or small business, a speaker bio, a short bio for a newsletter or event page.

## What stays the same

- Names, city, job title, business name, years in business, qualifications, awards.
- Links, handles, phone numbers, and booking details.
- Who is speaking. First person ("I bake cakes") stays first person. Third person ("Priya bakes cakes") stays third person, unless the user asks to switch.
- The facts about the person. Do not add hobbies, credentials, or personality traits they did not mention.

## What makes a bio sound generated

- Adjective piles. "Passionate, creative, driven storyteller." Say what the person does instead.
- Vague missions. "On a mission to inspire and uplift." Inspire who, to do what?
- Pipe-separated label lists. "Dreamer | Doer | Coffee lover | Wanderer". Fine in very small doses on Instagram if the user likes it, but usually one plain line works better.
- Fake quirkiness. "Fluent in sarcasm", "Will probably steal your fries". If the user wrote it, it is theirs, keep it. Do not add it.
- Corporate About pages. "We are a customer-centric company committed to delivering excellence." Say what the business makes or does, for whom, and where.

## Steps by type

**Instagram bio** (about 150 characters, check `lists/platform-limits.md`).
1. One line on what they do or post about.
2. Optional second line: city, a small personal detail they gave, or what to expect.
3. Call to action or link line if they had one.
Count the characters before you finish.

**Dating profile.**
1. Keep it warm and specific. Details the user gave ("I make a decent biryani and a terrible omelette") beat lists of traits.
2. Leave room for conversation. A question or a concrete interest gives people something to reply to.
3. Never make the person sound like someone they are not. No invented hobbies, height, job, or values.

**Website About page.**
1. First paragraph: who, what, where. "Mehta Tailors has stitched suits and sherwanis in Jaipur since 1998."
2. Second paragraph: how they work or what makes them different, from facts the user gave.
3. Last line: how to get in touch or visit.
Keep it short. Most visitors read the first paragraph and stop.

**Speaker or event bio.** Third person, three to five sentences, most relevant facts first. End with something human only if the user provided it.

## Output

The bio, ready to paste. For Instagram, give the character count. If useful, offer one shorter variant. Then the change summary.

## Example

Before (small business About page):

```
At Sunrise Home Bakes, we are passionate about crafting unforgettable
culinary experiences. Our mission is to bring joy to every celebration
through our commitment to quality, creativity, and excellence. With a
customer-first approach, we strive to exceed expectations every time.
```

After:

```
Sunrise Home Bakes is a home bakery in Kochi run by Divya Menon. She makes
custom birthday cakes, cupcakes, and eggless brownies to order, and has been
baking from her kitchen since 2019.

Orders need two days' notice. Message on WhatsApp to place one.
```

What I changed
- Replaced the general mission line with what you actually bake and where.
- Used the details from your message: Kochi, eggless brownies, two days' notice, since 2019.
- Kept it about the same length.

Every fact in the "After" came from the user's message. If they had only sent the "Before", you would ask what they bake and where before rewriting, or keep it general.
