# Messages to teachers and schools

Help parents write to their child's school: a note to the class teacher, an absence explanation, a message in the class WhatsApp group, a PTA message, or a question to the school office.

For a formal leave letter in the Indian letter format, use `writing/formal-letters.md`. This module covers the everyday notes and messages around it.

## When this applies

- "Write a note to my son's teacher that he was sick yesterday."
- "Reply in the class group about the project materials."
- "Ask the teacher why my daughter's marks were cut."
- "Message for the PTA group about the annual day costumes."
- The user pastes a draft that sounds too stiff, too angry, or too long.

## What stays the same

- The child's name, class, section, and roll number as the parent gives them.
- Dates of absence, reasons the parent gives, subjects, test names, marks, and fees or amounts.
- The parent's request or question.
- How the parent addresses the teacher ("Ma'am", "Sir", "Mrs. Iyer", "Miss"). Schools differ, so keep what the parent uses.

Do not invent a medical reason, a doctor's note, or a family event. If the parent says "she was unwell", keep "unwell".

## Tone

- Respectful and short. Teachers read dozens of these.
- Friendly in WhatsApp groups, but not chatty. No forwarded-message style, no long greetings.
- For a concern or complaint, calm and specific: what happened, what you'd like to understand, and a request for a time to talk. Keep blame out. A parent who sounds reasonable gets a better hearing.
- Never share other children's names or details in a group message. If the parent's draft does, suggest moving it to a private message to the teacher.

## Steps

1. Lead with who the message is about: "This is about Aarav Shah, Class 4B."
2. Say the point in one or two sentences: the absence and its dates, the question, or the request.
3. Add only what the teacher needs: when the child will be back, whether they need the missed work, what the parent will send.
4. For a concern, ask a question rather than making a charge: "Could you help me understand how the marks for the science project were given?"
5. Close with a simple thanks and the parent's name and relationship ("Regards, Neha Shah (Aarav's mother)").
6. Keep a WhatsApp group reply to one or two lines.

## Output

The note or message, ready to send or write in the school diary, then a short **What I changed** list. For a very short message, one line of explanation is enough.

## Example

Before:

> Respected Ma'am, I am writing this to inform you that my son was not able to attend school yesterday due to the fact that he was not keeping well and had fever. Kindly excuse him for the same. Also please let me know what he missed and kindly do the needful. Thanking you in anticipation.

After:

> Good morning Ma'am, Aarav (4B) was absent yesterday, 18 July, because he had a fever. He's better now and back today. Could you let me know if there's any classwork or homework he should catch up on? Thank you. Neha Shah (Aarav's mother)

What I changed
- Put his name, class, and the date at the start.
- Cut "due to the fact that", "do the needful", and "thanking you in anticipation".
- Kept the reason exactly as you gave it: fever.

(The date and class came from the parent's message in this example. Without them, leave `[class]` and `[date]` as blanks.)
