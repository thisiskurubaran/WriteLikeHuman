# Formal letters (Indian formats)

## When this applies

Leave applications, complaint letters, letters to a bank, government office, school principal, college, housing society, police station, or electricity board. Also no-objection requests, address change requests, and similar letters written in the standard Indian format.

## What stays the same

- The format itself. Indian offices expect a fixed layout, so keep it. Do not turn a formal letter into an email-style note.
- Every name, designation, address, account number, roll number, consumer number, date, amount, and reference number.
- Legal or official terms the user used: "FIR", "NOC", "KYC", "affidavit", "notarised", section numbers.
- "Respected Sir/Madam" and "Yours faithfully" or "Yours sincerely" if the user used them. These are normal in India, not stiff.

## The format

```
From
<Name>
<Address line 1>
<Address line 2>
<Phone number>

Date: <DD Month YYYY>

To
<Designation>
<Office or organisation name>
<Address>

Subject: <What the letter is about, in one line>

Respected Sir/Madam,

<Paragraph 1: who you are and what you are asking for.>

<Paragraph 2: the details. Dates, numbers, what happened.>

<Paragraph 3: what you want done, and by when if there is a deadline.>

Thanking you,

Yours faithfully,
<Signature>
<Name>
```

Notes on the format:

- **Yours faithfully** when writing to an office, a designation, or someone you do not know by name ("The Branch Manager"). **Yours sincerely** when writing to a named person you know ("Dear Mrs. Iyer").
- School leave letters often put "To" first and "From" after the body, or skip "From" for a student. Keep whatever layout the user's school uses.
- "Thanking you" before the closing is common and fine. Keep it if the user has it.
- If the user needs an enclosures line ("Enclosures: copy of Aadhaar, copy of passbook"), keep it after the signature.

## What to fix

Indian formal letters often pick up phrases that sound copied from a template. Fix the ones that hide the meaning, and leave the conventions alone.

| Stiff | Plainer, still formal |
|---|---|
| I beg to state that | I am writing to tell you that, or just start with the point |
| Kindly do the needful | Please update my address in your records (say what the needful is) |
| I humbly request your good self | I request you |
| It is to bring to your kind notice that | I would like to report that |
| Please revert at the earliest | Please reply by <date> (only if the user gave a date) |

Also:

- Make the subject line specific. "Subject: Request for leave" becomes "Subject: Leave for 12 and 13 March due to fever".
- Keep the body to three short paragraphs where possible. Officials skim.
- Remove repetition. Many letters say the same request three times.
- Keep it polite but direct. A complaint letter can be firm without being rude.

## Steps

1. Identify the letter type and the reader's designation.
2. Check the layout against the format above. Fix the order only if something is clearly missing or misplaced. Do not add fake addresses or phone numbers: leave a placeholder like `<your phone number>` if the user did not give one.
3. Rewrite the subject line to say exactly what the letter is about.
4. Rewrite the body for clarity, keeping every fact.
5. Check the closing (faithfully or sincerely).
6. Re-check against `ai-patterns.md` and write the change summary.

## Output

The full letter, laid out and ready to print or paste. If the user sent a .docx, follow `files/word.md` and keep their file layout. Then the change summary.

## Example

Before:

```
Subject: Leave application

Respected Sir,

I beg to state that I am a student of your esteemed school studying in class
8-B. It is to bring to your kind notice that I was suffering from high fever
and therefore I could not attend the school. I humbly request your good self
to kindly grant me leave for 2 days. I shall be highly obliged.

Thanking you,
Yours obediently,
Ananya Kulkarni
Class 8-B, Roll No. 17
```

After:

```
Subject: Leave for 4 and 5 September due to fever

Respected Sir,

I am Ananya Kulkarni of class 8-B. I could not attend school on 4 and
5 September because I had a high fever.

I request you to grant me leave for these two days.

Thanking you,
Yours obediently,
Ananya Kulkarni
Class 8-B, Roll No. 17
```

What I changed
- Made the subject line say which days and why.
- Replaced "I beg to state" and "your good self" with plain wording.
- Kept "Yours obediently", which schools still expect from students.
- Kept your name, class, and roll number as written.

The dates in the "After" come from the user's message. If they had only said "2 days", the rewrite would say "for two days" and leave a `<dates>` placeholder in the subject.
