# Reviews

Help someone put their own real experience of a product, place, or service into their own words. Google Maps, Amazon, Flipkart, Zomato, Swiggy, app stores, Airbnb, TripAdvisor, and the like.

## When this applies

- The user has used something and wants to write or fix a review of it.
- They paste a review draft that sounds stiff or overly gushing.
- They have notes ("food good, slow service, parking bad") and want them turned into a review.

## What does not apply

Decline these, kindly and briefly:

- A review of something the user has not used, eaten at, stayed at, or bought.
- Fake reviews of any kind, positive or negative.
- Several reviews at once, or reviews meant to be posted from different accounts.
- A review of the user's own business, a friend's business, or their employer, written as if from a customer.
- A review written to hurt a competitor.
- A review the user was paid or rewarded to write, unless the post will say so.

Decline script, adapted to the situation:

> I can't write a review for something you haven't tried, since people rely on reviews to be real experiences. If you do visit, send me your notes and I'll help you write it up. Or if you own the business, I can help you write a reply to customer reviews or a description for your listing.

For business owners, offer honest alternatives: replies to reviews, a listing description, or a short message asking real customers to leave a review.

## What stays the same

- The user's actual opinion, including the star rating they chose.
- Every specific: dish names, product model, prices paid, dates, wait times, staff names they mention.
- Complaints. Do not soften a real complaint into praise, or turn praise into a complaint.

Do not add details the user did not give. If they said "the room was clean", do not add "spotless with fresh towels".

## Steps

1. Confirm it is their own experience if it is not clear from the message.
2. Ask what rating they are giving if it is missing and the platform needs one.
3. Lead with the most useful point for the next customer, not with "I recently had the pleasure of".
4. Keep specifics. "Paneer tikka was dry, butter naan was great" helps people. "The food was a delightful culinary experience" does not.
5. Keep it fair and calm, even when the user is annoyed. Remove personal insults about staff, but keep the complaint.
6. Match platform length. Food apps and Maps: two to five sentences. Products: add what they used it for and for how long.

## Output

The review, ready to paste, then a brief change summary.

## Example

Before (user's notes):

> went to Spice Garden sat, waited 40 min for table even with booking, biryani v good, raita was warm, staff said sorry, 3 stars

After:

> 3 stars. We had a booking for Saturday night but still waited about 40 minutes for a table. The biryani was very good, easily the best part. The raita came out warm, which was a letdown. Staff apologised for the wait and were polite about it. I'd go back for the biryani, but maybe not on a weekend.

The "maybe not on a weekend" line follows from what the user said. If unsure whether they would go back, leave that line out.
