# Marriage biodata

Help someone write or tidy an Indian marriage biodata, or the "about me" text on a matrimony site like Shaadi, BharatMatrimony, or Jeevansathi. Often a parent writes it for their son or daughter.

The goal is warm and factual. A biodata is read by families who want clear details and a sense of the person, not a sales pitch.

## When this applies

- The user pastes a biodata, or a list of details, and asks to make it sound better or more natural.
- They want the "about me" or "about my family" paragraph for a matrimony profile.
- A parent is writing on behalf of their child.

## What stays the same

- Every detail exactly as given: name, date and time of birth, height, education, college, job, company, salary or income, city, family members and what they do.
- Fields the user chose to include, such as religion, caste, sub-caste, gotra, rashi, nakshatra, manglik status, or horoscope details. Keep them as written, with no comment or judgement. Do not add these fields if the user left them out.
- The order of sections, if the user already has a layout their family expects.
- Who is writing. If a parent writes "our daughter", keep that voice. If the person writes about themselves, keep "I".

Never inflate or invent. Do not round "5 ft 5 in" up to "5 ft 6 in", turn "graduate" into "postgraduate", add a salary that was not given, or describe the family as "well settled" or "respected" unless the user said so. Families check these details, and a wrong one causes real trouble later.

## Things to handle gently

- Skin tone and fairness words ("fair", "wheatish", "fair complexion girl wanted"). If they appear, suggest dropping them in one short, kind line: "Many families now leave out complexion. Want me to remove it?" If the user wants it kept, keep their wording.
- Demands that read badly, like dowry hints ("decent marriage expected"). Point them out once, plainly, and let the user decide.
- Health details. Keep only what the user chose to share.

## Steps

1. Sort the details into the usual sections: personal details, education and work, family, about the person, and expectations or partner preferences. Keep any extra section the user has.
2. Keep the factual sections as clean lists: "Height: 5 ft 4 in", "Education: B.Com, Ethiraj College, Chennai". No adjectives in the fact lines.
3. Write the "about" paragraph in plain, warm sentences. Use what the user told you about the person: hobbies, habits, values, what they enjoy. Two to five sentences.
4. Write expectations simply and respectfully: "We are looking for someone kind, educated, and working in Bangalore or Chennai." Avoid long lists of demands.
5. Remove matrimony clichés that say nothing: "homely yet modern", "perfect blend of traditional and modern values", "god-fearing and family-oriented" unless the user truly means each word.
6. Check every number and name against the original before returning it.

## Output

The biodata, ready to paste or print, with the fact sections as lists and the about paragraph as short prose. Then a brief **What I changed** list. If you left blanks for missing details, list them in one line.

## Example

Before (written by a parent):

> Our daughter Divya is a homely yet modern girl with a perfect blend of traditional and modern values. She is fair, slim and beautiful. She is a highly qualified professional working in a reputed MNC. She is god-fearing, family-oriented and believes in simple living and high thinking.

After:

> Divya works as a software tester at Corvan Systems in Pune, where she has been for three years. She likes cooking on weekends, plays badminton most evenings, and is close to her younger brother. She is calm, organised, and enjoys a quiet evening at home as much as a trip with friends.

What I changed
- Replaced the general phrases with details you gave me: her job, her hobbies, her brother.
- Left out "fair, slim and beautiful". Many families now skip looks and complexion. Tell me if you want it back.
- Kept "Corvan Systems" and "three years" exactly as you wrote them.

(The job, hobbies, and family detail in the "after" came from the parent's notes in this example. Without them, ask one question: "What does she enjoy doing?")
