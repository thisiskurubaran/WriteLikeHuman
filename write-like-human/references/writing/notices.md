# Newsletters and community notices

Help people write notices and short newsletters that others actually read: housing society circulars, school PTA updates, club newsletters, office notice boards, and RWA announcements.

## When this applies

- A society secretary, PTA member, club organiser, or office admin has a notice or newsletter draft.
- The draft is long, stiff, or reads like a government circular when it is going to a WhatsApp group.
- They have bullet notes and want a clean notice.

## What stays the same

- Dates, days, times, and deadlines.
- Amounts: maintenance charges, fines, fees, contributions, with currency.
- Rules and their exact conditions ("no parking in the visitor area after 10 pm").
- Names of committee members, flats, wings, classes, and venues.
- Any line quoted from a bylaw, circular, or official order.
- The sender and their role.

## Steps

1. Work out the main point. Most notices have one: something is changing, something is due, or something is happening.
2. Put that point in the subject or first line. "Water supply off on Saturday 10 am to 2 pm" beats "Important Notice Regarding Water".
3. Give the details people need to act: what, when, where, how much, who to contact.
4. Use short paragraphs or a small list for several items. Bold only the one thing people must not miss, if anything.
5. Cut stock lines: "This is to bring to the kind notice of all residents", "Your kind cooperation is highly solicited", "Members are hereby informed".
6. Keep it polite and firm for rules and dues. No sarcasm, no shouting in capitals, no naming and shaming specific flats unless the committee insists, and then keep it factual.
7. For newsletters, lead with what is most useful to readers, then the rest. Short headed sections are fine here.
8. Check every date, amount, and name against the draft.

## Formats

A printed notice board notice can keep the formal shape: title, date, body, signed by the secretary with their role. A WhatsApp version should be shorter and drop the formal frame. Offer both if the user is posting in both places.

## Output

The notice or newsletter, ready to post, then a short change summary.

## Example

Before:

> NOTICE
>
> This is to bring to the kind notice of all the esteemed residents of Green Park CHS that it has been decided in the Managing Committee meeting held on 2nd March that the annual maintenance charges for the financial year 2025-26 shall be revised to Rs 3,200 per month w.e.f. 1st April. All members are hereby requested to kindly make the payment before the 10th of every month failing which a penalty of Rs 200 shall be levied. Your kind cooperation is highly solicited.
>
> Secretary

After:

> Maintenance charges from 1 April
>
> At the committee meeting on 2 March, we agreed to revise maintenance to Rs 3,200 a month for 2025-26, starting 1 April.
>
> Please pay by the 10th of each month. Late payments will have a Rs 200 penalty.
>
> Questions? Message the secretary.
> Secretary, Green Park CHS

What I changed
- Put the change in the title so people see it right away.
- Cut "bring to the kind notice" and "cooperation is highly solicited".
- Split the long sentence into two short ones.
- Kept the amounts, dates, and the penalty exactly as you had them.
