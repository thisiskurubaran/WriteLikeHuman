# Eulogies and obituaries

Help someone write words about a person who has died: a eulogy or speech for a funeral, memorial, or prayer meeting (chautha, uthamakriya, shraddh, a church service), or an obituary or death notice for a newspaper, WhatsApp, or social media.

People asking for this are grieving and often short on time. Be gentle, be brief in your own replies, and keep the focus on the person who died.

For condolence messages to someone else who is grieving, use `writing/wishes-cards.md`. For general speech pacing, see `writing/speeches.md`.

## When this applies

- "Help me write a eulogy for my grandfather."
- "Write a newspaper obituary for my mother."
- "We need a WhatsApp message about my uncle's passing and the prayer meeting."
- The user pastes a draft and wants it to read better.

## What stays the same

- The person's full name as the family writes it, including titles, initials, and "Late" or "Smt." or "Shri" if used.
- Age, dates of birth and death, places, and the names and relationships of family members.
- Prayer meeting, funeral, or memorial details: date, day, time, venue, and address, exactly.
- Every memory, quality, and story the user gives, in their words as far as possible.

Only use real memories from the user. Never invent an anecdote, a saying, a hobby, an achievement, or a quality. If the user gives none, ask gently for one or two: "Is there a small memory or habit of hers you'd like people to remember?" If they would rather not, write a simple, short version without one.

## Care with grief

- Simple and sincere. No jokes unless the user gave a funny memory and wants it included, and then keep it warm.
- No forced positivity ("Everything happens for a reason", "She's in a better place") unless the user uses those words themselves or they match the family's faith.
- Respect religious and cultural wording the family uses: "passed away", "left for heavenly abode", "attained the lotus feet of", "Om Shanti", "RIP", "Inna lillahi wa inna ilayhi raji'un". Do not swap these for your own.
- Do not mention the cause of death unless the user includes it.

## Eulogy steps

1. Ask, if needed, how long it should be. About 130 words a minute when spoken; most eulogies are two to five minutes.
2. Open with who the person was to the speaker: "My grandfather, Raghunath Joshi, was the first person I called with good news."
3. Share two or three real memories or qualities. Short scenes work better than lists of virtues.
4. Write for speaking: short sentences, natural pauses, nothing hard to say through tears.
5. End simply: a thank you, a line about what they leave behind, or a goodbye in the family's own words.

## Obituary and notice format

A common newspaper or WhatsApp shape:

```
With deep sorrow we inform the passing of
[Name], aged [age], on [date], at [place].

[One or two lines about the person, from the family.]

Survived by [family members as the user lists them].

Prayer meeting: [day, date], [time], at [venue, address].

[Family names or "The [surname] family"]
```

Keep it short for newspapers, where space costs money. Check the newspaper's word or line limit if the user mentions one.

## Output

The eulogy or notice, ready to read or send, then a very short **What I changed** list. In grief, keep your own words few.

## Example

Before (WhatsApp notice):

> It is with profound grief and a heavy heart that we inform you all that our beloved father, a pillar of strength and a beacon of light for the entire family, Shri Mohan Lal Verma, has left for his heavenly abode on 3rd October 2025 at the age of 78. He touched countless lives. Prayer meeting will be held on 6th October, Monday, 4-5 pm at Arya Samaj Mandir, Sector 15.

After:

> With deep sorrow we share that our father, Shri Mohan Lal Verma, left for his heavenly abode on 3 October 2025, aged 78.
>
> Prayer meeting: Monday, 6 October, 4 pm to 5 pm, Arya Samaj Mandir, Sector 15.
>
> The Verma family

What I changed
- Kept his name, age, dates, and the prayer meeting details exactly.
- Kept "left for his heavenly abode", since that's your family's wording.
- Shortened the opening so the details are easy to find.
