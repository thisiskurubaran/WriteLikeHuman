# Freelance proposals and quotes

Help freelancers and small agencies pitch for work and send quotes: Upwork and Fiverr proposals, cold pitches, replies to client briefs, and price quotes.

## When this applies

- A freelancer has a proposal draft that sounds like every other one ("I am a highly skilled professional with extensive experience").
- They paste a job post or client brief and want a reply.
- They need a quote or estimate written clearly.

## What stays the same

- Prices, rates, currency, payment terms, advance percentages, and taxes.
- Timelines and delivery dates.
- Scope: what is included, what is not, number of revisions.
- The freelancer's real experience, past clients, portfolio links, tools, and credentials.
- Client names and project details from the brief.

## Never invent

- Past clients, brands, or projects.
- Portfolio items, case studies, or results ("increased sales by 40 percent").
- Ratings, reviews, Top Rated badges, or years of experience.
- Certifications, degrees, or tools they did not mention.

If a strong proposal needs an example and the user did not give one, ask: "Do you have a past project that's close to this one?" A short honest proposal beats an impressive made up one, and invented claims can get an account banned.

## Steps

1. Read the client's brief first. Find what they actually need and any question they asked.
2. Open with the client's problem, not the freelancer's life story. "You need your Shopify store's product pages rewritten before the Diwali sale" shows they read the brief.
3. Say how the freelancer would do it, in two or three concrete steps.
4. Add one relevant past example, if the user gave one, with a link.
5. Answer any screening questions directly.
6. Give price and timeline clearly, or say what they need to know before quoting.
7. End with one simple next step: a question or a call suggestion. No "Looking forward to a long-term fruitful association".
8. Keep it short. Most clients skim the first two lines. Upwork shows only the start of a proposal before "more".

## Quotes

For a quote, use a small table or list: item, quantity, rate, amount, then total, taxes, and payment terms. Keep every number exact. Add what is not included in one line. Check the maths, and if the user's numbers do not add up, point it out instead of fixing them silently.

## Output

The proposal or quote, ready to paste, then a short change summary. List anything the user should add, like a portfolio link.

## Example

Before (Upwork):

> Dear Hiring Manager, I am a highly skilled and experienced content writer with a passion for creating engaging and high-quality content. I have worked with numerous clients across various industries and I am confident that I am the perfect fit for your project. I look forward to hearing from you.

Client brief: "Need 10 blog posts on home gardening for Indian balconies, 800 words each, SEO basics."

Freelancer's notes: "2 years writing, did 15 gardening posts for GreenNest blog, link: [portfolio link], Rs 1,500 per post, 10 days"

After:

> Hi, I can write your 10 balcony gardening posts. I've written 15 gardening pieces for the GreenNest blog, including two on growing herbs in small spaces: [portfolio link]
>
> I'd plan the 10 topics with you first, then send two posts at a time so you can give feedback early. Each post would be about 800 words with a keyword in the title and headings.
>
> Rs 1,500 per post, Rs 15,000 for all 10, delivered within 10 days.
>
> Do you already have a list of topics, or should I suggest some?

What I changed
- Opened with the client's project instead of a general introduction.
- Used your real GreenNest work as the example.
- Added the total so the client doesn't have to work it out.
- Ended with a question to start the conversation.

(The "two on growing herbs" detail is shown as if the freelancer mentioned it. In real use, only include what they told you.)
