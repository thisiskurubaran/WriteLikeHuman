# Replies to customer reviews

Help business owners answer reviews of their own business, as themselves. Google Maps, Zomato, Swiggy, Amazon, Flipkart, Justdial, app stores, and the like.

This is the honest side of reviews. Owners cannot write reviews of their own business (see `writing/reviews.md`), but they can and should reply to real ones.

## When this applies

- A shop, restaurant, clinic, salon, homestay, or seller wants to reply to a customer review.
- They paste a draft reply that sounds defensive, robotic, or copy-pasted.
- They paste a review and ask "what should I say?"

## What does not apply

Decline these, kindly and briefly, and offer the honest reply instead:

- Writing a reply that pretends to be from a customer, or posting "replies" from fake accounts.
- Offering refunds, discounts, or gifts in exchange for changing or deleting a review.
- Threatening the reviewer, or hinting at legal action in a public reply.
- Replies that reveal the customer's private details: phone number, address, order history, what they bought, medical details.

## What stays the same

- The owner's facts: what happened, what they will do, refund or replacement terms they actually offer.
- Names the owner uses for the business, dishes, products, and staff.
- Order numbers only if the owner wants them in a public reply. Usually suggest moving those to a private message instead.
- The owner's position. If they think the review is unfair, the reply can say so calmly. Do not turn it into a grovelling apology they did not mean.

## Steps

1. Read the review. Find the one or two specific points the customer raised.
2. Thank them by name if the platform shows a name. Keep it to one short line.
3. Answer the specific point. "Sorry the biryani was cold" beats "Sorry for any inconvenience".
4. Say what the owner will do, if they told you. Do not promise fixes they did not mention.
5. For complaints, offer a private channel for details: "Please message us on the number on our page so we can sort it out." Use the owner's real contact route, or leave `[contact]` as a blank.
6. For good reviews, keep it short and warm. Mention something they liked. Do not turn it into an ad.
7. Remove defensive lines ("We have never had this complaint before", "You must be mistaken"). Keep a calm correction if a fact in the review is wrong.
8. Keep it short. Two to four sentences for most replies. Other customers read these too.
9. Vary replies. If the owner replies to many reviews, do not give every one the same template. Copy-paste replies read as cold.

## Output

The reply, ready to paste. Then a short change summary. If the owner gave several reviews, give one reply for each, each one specific to that review.

## Example

Review (3 stars, from "Ankit S."):

> Food was good but delivery took almost an hour and the gulab jamun was missing from my order.

Owner's draft:

> Dear Valued Customer, we sincerely apologize for any inconvenience caused. Customer satisfaction is our top priority. We assure you this will not happen again. Thank you for choosing us!

After:

> Thanks for the honest review, Ankit, and sorry about the wait and the missing gulab jamun. Friday nights were short-staffed and we've added a delivery partner since. Please message us on the number on our page and we'll make it right.

What I changed
- Used the reviewer's name and the two problems they mentioned.
- Swapped the general apology for a specific one.
- Kept the staffing reason and the new delivery partner, which you told me about.
- Added a private route for the missing item instead of discussing the order in public.
