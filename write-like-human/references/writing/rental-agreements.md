# Rental agreements in plain words

Explain a tenant or landlord agreement in plain words, or help draft one, without changing what it means.

## When this applies

- "What does this rental agreement actually say?", "explain this clause", "is anything unusual in here?"
- "Help me write a simple rental agreement for my flat."
- Leave and licence agreements, PG agreements, and renewal letters.

## This is not legal advice

Say this once, in one line, at the start: "I can explain this in plain words, but I'm not a lawyer. For anything you're unsure about, check with a lawyer or your local rules."

In India, mention that stamp duty and registration rules differ by state, and the user should check them (for example on their state's registration department website) before signing. Don't state amounts or rules for a specific state from memory.

## What stays the same

- Legal terms, clause numbers, amounts, rent, deposit, notice periods, dates, names, and addresses. Never change these.
- What each clause means. A plain explanation must not make a clause sound stricter or looser than it is.
- The original text. When explaining, keep the original clause next to your plain version so the user can compare.

## Steps

### Explaining an agreement

1. Go clause by clause, or the clauses the user asks about.
2. For each, show the original (short quote or clause number) and then a plain version in one or two sentences.
3. Flag things people commonly miss: lock-in periods, notice periods, deposit deductions, rent increase terms, who pays maintenance, and painting or repair clauses. Flag, don't judge.
4. If a clause is unclear or seems one-sided, say so plainly and suggest asking the landlord or a lawyer. Don't call it illegal.

### Drafting an agreement

1. Use only the facts the user gives: names, address, rent, deposit, dates, notice period, who pays what.
2. Leave clear blanks for anything missing: `[security deposit amount]`.
3. Keep clauses short and numbered, in plain words, without dropping the legal meaning.
4. Remind the user to check stamp duty and registration rules, and to have a lawyer look if it matters.

## Output

- Explaining: a list of clauses, each with the original and a plain version, then the flagged points.
- Drafting: the agreement with blanks, ready to review, and the one-line reminder about legal checks.

## Example

Original clause:

> 7. The Licensee shall not be entitled to a refund of the security deposit in the event of termination prior to the expiry of the lock-in period of 11 (eleven) months.

Plain version:

> Clause 7: If you leave before the first 11 months are up, you won't get your security deposit back.

Worth checking: this is a strict lock-in. Ask the landlord whether a shorter notice with partial refund is possible before you sign.
