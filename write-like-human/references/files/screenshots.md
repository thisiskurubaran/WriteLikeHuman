# Screenshots of chats and emails

## When this applies

The user sends a picture instead of text: a WhatsApp screenshot from their landlord, a photo of an email on a laptop screen, a screenshot of a society group message, or a text from a delivery person. Usually they want a reply drafted ("what do I say to this?") or their own draft in the screenshot fixed.

Load `writing/chat-replies.md` for WhatsApp and SMS, or `writing/email.md` for emails, together with this module.

## What stays the same

- The facts in the conversation: amounts, dates, times, flat numbers, order numbers, and what each person asked for.
- Who said what. In chat screenshots, messages on the right are usually the user's and messages on the left are the other person's. If it is not clear, ask.
- The language and script of the chat. If the landlord writes in Hinglish, the reply can be in Hinglish.

## Privacy

Screenshots often show more than the user meant to share: phone numbers, other people's names and photos, other chats in the list, notification banners, bank balances.

- Use only what you need for the reply.
- Do not repeat phone numbers, email addresses, account numbers, or other people's personal details back in your answer.
- Do not comment on unrelated things you can see (other chats, notifications, photos).
- If the screenshot contains something sensitive that the reply does not need, you can suggest in one line that they crop it next time.

## Steps

1. Read the screenshot. Claude can read text in images directly. Work out who is who and what the last message asks for.
2. If any part needed for the reply is blurry, cut off, or too small to read, ask about that part. Do not guess an amount, a date, or a name.
3. If the screenshot shows the user's own draft that they want fixed, rewrite it following `ai-patterns.md` and the chat or email module.
4. If they want a reply, draft one that answers what was actually asked, in the tone of the conversation. A landlord's two line message gets a two line reply.
5. Match the platform: chat replies are short and can skip greetings, emails keep a greeting and sign-off.
6. Check every fact in the reply against the screenshot.

## Output

- The reply or rewritten text, ready to copy. For chats, plain text with no formatting, since bold and bullets do not paste well into WhatsApp.
- A short change summary only if you rewrote the user's own draft. For a fresh reply, at most one line about any choice they may want to change ("I kept it polite but firm about the date").

## Example

Screenshot (described): a WhatsApp chat. The landlord, on the left, writes: "Hi, plumber coming tomorrow 11am for bathroom leak. Someone shd be home pls." The user asks: "what do I reply, I have office."

Reply:

> Hi uncle, thanks for arranging it. I'll be at office tomorrow, but my neighbour in 3B has a spare key and can let him in. Could you ask him to call me when he reaches?

Only mention the neighbour and the spare key if the user said so. Otherwise draft: "I'll be at office tomorrow. Is there any chance he can come after 6, or on Saturday?" and offer to change it.

## If something goes wrong

- **The image is too blurry or cropped**: say which part you cannot read and ask for a clearer screenshot or the text pasted in.
- **It is unclear which side is the user**: ask "Which messages are yours, the green ones on the right?"
- **There are many screenshots of a long chat**: ask which message they want to reply to, or reply to the latest one and say so.
- **The screenshot is of a document, not a chat** (a letter, a form): treat the text as pasted text and use the matching writing module.
