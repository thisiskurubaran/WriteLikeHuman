# Team style guide

Use a shared house style for a team or small business, so everyone's writing sounds like the same company.

## When this applies

- The user attaches or pastes a style guide: "here's our house style", "use our brand voice", "follow our company style guide".
- The user wants to make one: "help me write a style guide for my bakery", "set up a writing style for my team".
- The blank form is `voice/style-guide-template.md`.

A voice profile is one person's way of writing. A style guide is a team's or a business's. A user can have both.

## What stays the same

- Facts, numbers, names, and dates, as always.
- Product names, brand names, and terms the guide lists are protected exactly as spelled there. Treat them like a list from `adjust/protected-words.md`.
- Quotes, legal text, and anything the user marks as fixed.

## Where it lives

The skill cannot store anything between chats. The team keeps the guide as a file or a shared note and attaches it when they want it used. Say so in one line the first time.

## Steps

### Building a guide

1. Show the blank form from `voice/style-guide-template.md` (its headings are enough) so they see what you'll fill in. Then ask for two or three pieces of writing the team is happy with, such as a good customer email, a product page, or a social post. Ask what they never want to sound like.
2. Fill in the template: tone, words to use and avoid, product names, greetings and sign-offs, formatting rules (date format, currency, capital letters in headings), and phrases customers respond well to.
3. Keep it short. One page is better than five. A guide nobody reads does nothing.
4. Show it to the user to check, then give it back as a file to save.

### Applying a guide

1. Read the guide before rewriting or drafting.
2. Follow it over the general advice in this skill. If the guide says "always sign off with Team Kiran Bakes", do that.
3. The user's explicit request in the current chat wins over the guide. If they say "make this one casual" and the guide says formal, make it casual and mention the difference in one line.
4. If the guide has a voice profile alongside it, use the guide for company rules (names, formats, banned words) and the profile for personal habits (sentence length, how direct they are).
5. In the change summary, mention when you changed something to match the guide: "Changed 'customers' to 'guests', as your style guide asks."

## Output

- Building: the filled-in guide as a file, ready to save and share with the team.
- Applying: the rewritten text or file, then the usual short change summary.

## Example

Style guide excerpt (Kiran Bakes): "We call people 'guests', not 'customers'. Say 'freshly baked', never 'premium'. Dates as 12 March. Sign off: Team Kiran Bakes."

Before:

> Dear valued customers, our premium cakes are now available for pre-order until 3/12. Thank you!

After:

> Hi everyone, our freshly baked cakes are open for pre-orders until 12 March. Thank you!
>
> Team Kiran Bakes

What I changed
- "Customers" is now "everyone", since the guide prefers not to say customers.
- Swapped "premium" for "freshly baked", as the guide asks.
- Wrote the date as 12 March and added your sign-off.
